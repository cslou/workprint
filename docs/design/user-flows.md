## 1. Purpose

Design an app that lets a creator direct a coherent animation through understandable creative decisions, rather than manage intermediate files, prompts and production bookkeeping.

Use **Mercer & Vale** as the concrete walkthrough. The current exercise starts with one Shot before expanding to a Narrative Scene, revisions and recovery paths. The specific broader Scene and first-release endpoint are not selected yet. Existing local Gallery scripts, reference images and storyboard iterations can supply walkthrough material; their existence does not prove the new flow works or approve them for a new use.

We are documenting the experience and inspecting exploratory layout mockups before committing to a complete screen inventory or implementing the app. A flow describes a user goal, what the user sees and does, what the agent handles, and what happens next—not necessarily a separate screen for each step.

## 2. Direction already agreed

These are intended design decisions from the catalogue and app-flow discussions, not claims that the current skills implement them.

| Topic | Agreed direction |
| --- | --- |
| Pre-production review | Two required creative reviews on the happy path: the complete asset catalogue, then the complete rendered storyboard |
| Intermediate work | Scene Script preparation, asset inventory, Shot planning and candidate inspection are agent-managed; no mandatory script or textual Shot-plan review in the intended happy path |
| Creative ambiguity | Ask when an unresolved choice materially changes the creator's intended story; fewer turns must not mean inventing intent |

## 3. Working user and scope

**Working user hypothesis:** a solo storyteller or independent creator adapting narrative material, consistent with the strategy one-pager. The primary segment, first-release scope and commercial assumptions remain unvalidated; do not silently treat this as completed product discovery.

**Working entry scenario:** the creator brings existing prose plus creative direction. Other entry types—raw idea, screenplay, existing storyboard or imported project—remain open app decisions, not promised capabilities.

**First-release endpoint: unresolved (Q-01).** Candidate endpoints are an approved storyboard, reviewed motion/Selects assembly, or a finished film. The current repository reaches reviewed Selects and handoff, not full editing, sound finishing or final delivery. Planning a finished-film app would add scope, not merely wrap existing functions.

The current design exercise is the single-scene Editor continuation (§4). Its entry/layout do not settle the first-release endpoint. Earlier whole-story navigation remains a saved reference, not a constraint on this exercise. Platform, account system, collaboration model, provider UI and app architecture remain open.

### Flow 1 — create one Shot in Generate

**Status: all nine steps shown as approved examples (D-48); runtime differences below still open.** Originally proposed at the creator's request (D-14). The creator drafted steps 1–9; the agent's suggestions for step 6, the panel count and generation details are marked as proposals. Example walkthrough (r09) (removed: `flow1-r09.html`).

**Goal:** from an idea typed into the chat, reach one chosen video Take the creator is happy with, making each creative decision once.

#### Steps

**Layout and interaction rules (current, D-18 as revised by D-51–D-76).** *Sidebar:* ChatGPT/Claude pattern — **New video** and **Library** (the account’s approved characters, places and props), then **Recents** (one conversation per video). *Right panel:* always present, including the opening screen, with fixed tabs **Script · Assets · Storyboard · Videos** (left to right in working order); each tab shows a one-line empty state until the agent first writes it, then a shimmering placeholder while it works, then its content. Script holds the story only (what happens, dialogue, a plain-words shot breakdown, look chips); Assets holds the scene’s characters, places and props; Storyboard lists Shots in order, each with its panels (numbered 1.1, 1.2…, captions carrying who is where), camera as text and an Edit link, and “View storyboard” opens the full board where camera pills and caption pencils live; Videos lists one row per Shot with its chosen or newest version, “Version history · N earlier” closed inside the row, and Play all during version comparison. Once a current edit exists, a scene snapshot with **Open editor** and **Export** sits above the **Shots** list (D-86). The panel **never switches tabs on its own**; a tab changed out of view gets a small dot, cleared when opened. *Chat:* one chat box at all times, identical in every state. While the agent works, Send becomes **Stop** and typing queues for its next turn. Within a turn the agent **works first** (compact activity lines such as “✓ Made 4 panels”, each linking to its tab, while the panel fills in; slow items show “Making…” placeholders), **then writes** its message, **then asks**. A question and its options (lettered suggested prompts, image tiles, or a plan) grow on top of the chat box inside the same outline; typing in the box answers, or asks something else and leaves the question waiting; × folds it to “1 question waiting · Show”. Options are suggested prompts about things already visible, with no descriptions. Approvals use the same pattern and approve the exact draft shown. Answered questions stay in the chat with the question above the answer (and collapsed “Plan / Frame / Request you approved” where something was approved). *States:* **Empty / Draft / Ready** (Chosen for videos); “Making…” is progress, not a state; no live badges. *Drafts vs choices (D-63):* a single draft the agent will act on is saved to the project and shown in the tabs; alternatives (options, looks) stay in the chat and only the pick is saved. Manual edits in the board are saved at once and handed to the agent on its next turn (D-54). *Copy:* use “video” and “version” for media and alternatives, and **Shots** for the scene list/count. Take remains a glossary/runtime term; a Shot is not an interchangeable name for every timeline clip. Visual style: venice.ai’s light colours and fonts as reference only. Example pages are on [Generate flows](generate-flows.html), with Flow 1, Flow 2 and a clickable Demo.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Asks what the creator wants to make | — | — | — |
| 2 | Breaks the description into moment, dialogue, camera, Look, **cast list** (characters, environment, props that matter) and panel count; echoes its reading as editable chips | Describes the Shot in their own words | Answers only a question that would change intent (e.g. whether Mercer is visible) | Script |

No approval step | Opens images larger; picks one or suggests changes (“B, but greyer”) | **Pick a look** | Assets (Empty until a look is picked; unpicked looks stay in the conversation)
| 5 | Makes the reference sheets from the picked looks and the user's photos, and shows **all sheets together** | Approves, or asks for changes to specific sheets | **Approve the sheets** (one review, not one per sheet) | Assets (Ready); also saved to Library |
| 6 | Confirms the sheets are saved, then plans the frames and shows the plan **inside the approval card** (“Make the opening frame?”): what each frame shows, its camera and look, and the individual sheet images it will use *(proposal)* | Reads the plan; can open any reference’s sheet | One decision: the plan makes sense (cost hidden for now) | Storyboard (frame Empty); Assets (Ready) |

| 8 | Prepares the video request inside the approval card (“Make the video?”): what happens, length, quality, shape, camera, and the frame it starts on; says plainly that the reference sheets aren’t sent (endpoint frames and references can’t be combined) and that any sound is a draft | Reads the request | Approve the request (cost hidden for now) | Takes (Take 1 Empty) |
| 9 | Generates a Take and reports what it checked (start frame, shape and quality, measured length) and flags what it can’t judge (sound, performance) | Watches; revises or chooses | **Choose the Take**, or ask for another (new approval card with a diff) | Takes (Draft until chosen); download |

Per-step detail:

- **Step 2 asks only material questions.** Fewer turns must not mean invented intent (§2 Creative ambiguity).
- **Step 3 combines upload and reuse.** Uploaded photos become references; a sheet is proposed when an item needs several views or will be reused. *Proposal:* for a one-Shot prop, the uploaded photo can serve directly as the reference.
- **Step 6 skips a separate text review** *(proposal)*. Images are easier to judge than descriptions, and panels are cheap compared with video. The plan is still visible before any spend, so a wrong plan can be stopped. The agent still asks when an ambiguity changes intent.
- **Panel count for one Shot is one or two** *(proposal)*: a first frame and an optional last frame, because video generation accepts zero to two endpoint frames and stops on three or more timed Panels. A moment needing more becomes several Shots, which is Studio territory.

#### Generation details: type it, see it, change it *(proposal)*

The creator types details in plain words (“monochrome, bird's-eye, 8 seconds”). The agent echoes its interpretation as chips on the brief and on every approval card; tapping a chip changes a value, and “+ angle / + lens / + lighting” suggestions reveal what can be controlled.

| Level | Examples | Where | Default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Look (whole Shot) | monochrome, palette, rendering style, aspect ratio | Brief · Look | Asked once in step 2 if unstated; may be saved as a reusable look |
| Panel (each image) | bird's-eye, close-up, lens, lighting, time of day | Chips under each panel's description | Proposed by the agent from the moment |
| Video (each Take) | duration, camera move, motion strength, resolution, audio | Chips on the video approval card | Taken from the panels and Look |

- Video chips offer only values the selected provider supports; an unsupported request (e.g. 30 seconds) is explained before spend, with alternatives.
- Changing the Look after panels exist marks those panels out of date and offers regeneration with a diff.
- **Editing outputs** reuses the chips plus a text box, and every edit makes a new version while keeping the old one. A panel edit regenerates with the changed chip. A changed generation request means a new Take. Editing existing material belongs to the [Editor direction](#edit-a-scene), not automatic regeneration. Regional image editing and timeline media processing are not current runtime capabilities.
- The chip vocabulary must come from verified provider capabilities before it is fixed.

#### Runtime differences to resolve before implementation

- Folding the panel plan into the spend approval (step 6) differs from today's separate Shot-plan approval before paid Panel generation.
- Starting from a chat description without an approved Scene Script and full storyboard differs from the current storyboard-then-Production path. The standalone image and video generators already accept direct requests.
- Step 4 generates the looks without asking first, and costs are not shown in the current examples. Today's image rules require showing the exact request, uploads and cost and getting authorization before each paid call; generating without that is a runtime change, not only a design choice.
- Per-call cost approvals (steps 4, 6, 8 and each revision) follow today's rules. A per-Shot budget remains a later option (§2 Generation autonomy).

#### Still open for Flow 1

- Loading, failure and interrupted states at each generating step (Q-06).
- Whether a saved Look is an account asset alongside characters and places.
- How the creator resumes a half-finished Shot from the Generate home.

### Flow 2 — create a scene with several shots

**Status: generation/review examples approved (D-69, D-78); single-scene Editor entry/layout integrated under D-86, with detailed behaviour still partial.** Same screen as Flow 1, one conversation per scene. Script and Assets cover the whole scene; the Storyboard and Videos tabs list Shots in order (no shot selector, D-51). A Shot holds any number of panels, numbered 1.1, 1.2…, whose captions carry who is where; the system combines each Shot's panels into one video (D-52). The Storyboard tab is read-only with an Edit link per Shot; editing (camera pills, caption pencils) happens in the full board (D-53); manual edits are saved at once and handed to the agent on its next turn (D-54). Independent of Flow 1 (D-57): a new user with an empty Library and no earlier videos, so Vale, Mercer, the gallery and the bench are set up as in Flow 1 steps 3–5.

**Goal:** from one description, reach a chosen Take for every Shot of a short scene, setting up its characters, place and props once and checking that the Shots cut together.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| 2 | Drafts the Script: what happens, a **shot breakdown** (each Shot named by what happens, in plain words, with dialogue and positions; no camera settings, panels or timings) and the look; lists what it needs to know (Vale, Mercer, the gallery, the bench); asks only intent-changing questions, then whether the script looks right | Describes the scene; reviews only the Script tab | Answer questions; **agree the script** | Script; Assets (four items Empty); Storyboard not yet planned |
| 3 | Asks about each item in turn (as Flow 1 step 3, “Asset 1 of 4”) | Uploads, describes or skips per item, or answers several at once | Source for each item | Assets |
| 4 | Makes three looks for each described or designed item and shows them in the picker (as Flow 1 step 4) | Picks one per item or suggests changes | **Pick the looks** | Assets |
| 5 | Makes the reference sheets and shows all together (as Flow 1 step 5) | Approves or asks for changes | **Approve the sheets** | Assets (Ready); saved to Library |
| 6 | Plans the storyboard and shows the plan **inside the approval card** (as Flow 1 step 6): per Shot, camera, length, planned panels with captions, and the asset images it uses | Reads the plan; may adjust it in the board | **Approve the plan and making the panels** | Storyboard (Shots with planned panels, Empty) |
| 7 | Makes the panels; reports what it checked and any differences; “Approve the storyboard?” with View storyboard | Reviews in the tab or board; edits in the board (pending until handed over) | **Approve the storyboard**, or regenerate changed Shots (one card) | Storyboard (Draft → Ready) |

- Steps 3–5 repeat Flow 1’s asset stage for four items; whether they can be shortened for several items (e.g. one card per kind) is open.
- **Order comes from the Storyboard**, never from when a Take was made; each Shot has at most one chosen Take, and earlier Takes stay in that Shot's history.
- **Runtime gaps:** combining three or more panels into one video (today's adapter takes at most two endpoint frames and produce-scene stops on three or more timed panels); several Shots in one Generate conversation.
- Open: whether shot 2 may reuse Flow 1's Take; costs are still hidden.

### Edit a scene

**Status: entry/layout direction reviewed; brand-book integration authorized, final integrated inspection pending (D-86).** Visual/state inventory and exact reference geometry: [Editor in the guide](design.md#editor) and [brand book](brand-book/screens.html#editor). This is not a new runtime skill or implemented finishing capability.

1. After choosing a version for each Shot, a current scene edit is shown in **Videos**: one snapshot, **3 shots · 13.4 s**, **Open editor →** and **Export**. The list below is **Shots**, retaining per-row version, duration and Chosen. No Draft/Kept pill, edit number, Watch button, Whole scene overlay or duplicate count is needed on the card.
2. **Open editor** opens the complete edit, not a separate editor per Shot. Keep the same conversation. The Editor is a full-window workspace within Generate; the four main tabs remain **Script · Assets · Storyboard · Videos**.
3. The reviewed layout has tools left, player centre, full-height conversation right and a timeline below the tools/player only. Compact timeline, collapsible Visual/Audio groups and scrolling control visible density. The default is expanded with both groups open. No Inspect/Agent modes or comparison banner.
4. **← Videos** returns to that scene's entry. Returning is not creative approval, export, or a requirement to rebuild the conversation. Leave/resume, unsaved/pending proposals and concurrency still need explicit design.
5. **Export** can be chosen directly from Videos; no token edit or mandatory Editor visit. Current settings are a draft illustration only. Render validation, progress, cancel/failure/retry and verified completion are not designed.

The intended editing loop combines direct controls and conversation, but detailed properties, commands, proposal review/apply, undo and persistence rules are still open. Do not adopt the older “Keep this edit” click-through as policy for every manual movement. Original video versions remain distinct from an edited composition; changing timing need not generate a new video.

Track roles are optional, not seven mandatory lanes. Separate dialogue and ambience in the example are assumptions; mixed generated audio must remain **Original audio** unless separate media actually exists. Added captions/title/SFX/music are placeholders, not chosen creative additions.

Selecting all shot versions does not finish picture, sound or delivery review. Demo screen 18 now says the first edit is ready **to review**, and connects to this entry. D-69's earlier direct-download ending is superseded for this current-edit continuation, not retroactively erased from the historical record. Multi-scene editing is explicitly deferred until this integration is complete.

### Flow 3 — revise a Take

**Status: proposed.** Shown in r05 (Revise panel and diff), r06 and r08.

| # | Agent | Creator | Creator decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Shows the Take with its checks and the brief's must-haves | Watches it | — |
| 2 | — | Types feedback, or attaches the Take and says what's wrong | — |
| 3 | Turns the feedback into a change to the request and shows it as a diff inside the approval card; everything unchanged is listed as unchanged | Reads the diff and cost | Approve the new Take, edit the change, or cancel |
| 4 | Generates the new Take; the previous one stays in history | Compares if needed | **Choose**, revise again, or reject |

- **Reject** asks which must-have failed, so the reason is kept.
- **Choose** accepts a failed technical check (e.g. 6.4 s against a 6 s limit) only when the creator explicitly says so.
- Compare puts two Takes side by side against the same must-haves.

### Flow 4 — edit a panel or generation detail

**Status: proposed** (see *Generation details* under Flow 1).

1. The creator taps **Edit** on a panel or Take, or types the change (“make it bird's-eye”).
2. The agent shows the chips with the changed value and a one-line diff inside the approval card.
3. The creator approves the cost. The output is regenerated as a **new version**, and the old one is kept.
4. A changed panel marks any Take made from it as out of date. The agent offers a new Take; it never swaps it silently.

Changes to the generated performance or generation request make a new Take. Timing changes using existing material belong to the [Editor direction](#edit-a-scene); do not automatically route every change to regeneration. Neither regional image editing nor timeline processing is implemented by the current repository.

### Flow 5 — reuse a saved asset

**Status: account-level assets agreed (D-13); flow proposed.**

1. In Flow 1 step 3, the agent finds a matching saved asset and offers it: “Use Vale v2 from Your assets?” — alongside upload, describe, design or not needed.
2. The creator accepts, picks another version, or chooses a different source.
3. The creator can also @-mention an asset in any message, or link it from the side panel.
4. A reused asset skips the sheet steps (4–5) unless the creator asks for changes.

### Flow 6 — change an asset

**Status: proposed.** Shown in r06 (coat open vs scar) and r08.

| Change | Example | What happens | Creator decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| For this Shot only | “coat open”, “wet hair” | A note on this Shot's link to the asset; the asset itself doesn't change | None beyond the next generation approval |
| To the asset itself | “Vale gets a scar” | The agent proposes a **new version** (v3), lists every Shot using the old version, and shows which chosen Takes would become out of date | Approve making the version (cost). Then decide, per Shot or project, whether to move to it |

The agent decides which kind of change was meant and asks when it's unclear. Nothing moves to a new version until the creator says so, including Shots in the other product.

### Flow 7 — work on a Shot inside Studio

**Status: agreed direction (D-13); details proposed.**

1. From the Studio storyboard, the creator opens a Shot card.
2. The same Shot workspace as Generate opens: the conversation plus Spec · Frames · Assets · Takes.
3. Studio adds, around it:
   - a breadcrumb back to the storyboard;
   - a filmstrip of neighbouring Shots;
   - Context in the Spec (script, and the Shots before and after);
   - storyboard panels as the Frames;
   - the chosen Take feeding the sequence preview.
4. The creator works through Flow 1 steps 7–9 and Flow 3 as in Generate. The cast comes from the project's picks from Your assets.
5. The creator can attach a neighbouring Shot's frame to ask about continuity; the agent answers with both images side by side.

### Flow 8 — change across Shots

**Status: proposed.** Shown in r07 and r08.

1. In the Studio's project conversation, the creator asks for a change that spans Shots (“Vale gets a scar in every Shot”).
2. The agent shows one approval card with:
   - the change as a diff;
   - thumbnails of every affected Shot, and what happens to each (out of date, retry, unaffected);
   - the cost, and that Generate Shots stay on the old version.
3. The creator approves, then moves Shots to the new version and regenerates them through Flow 3.

A request made inside one Shot's conversation that affects other Shots gets the same card.

### Flow 9 — review the sequence

**Status: proposed.** Shown in r07 and r08.

1. The Studio storyboard shows a sequence preview on top: the chosen Takes in Shot order, with storyboard panels standing in for Shots without a Take.
2. The creator plays it, and sees each cut marked as OK, “no Take yet” or “failed”.
3. From a gap or failure, the creator opens that Shot (Flow 7).

This older Studio proposal did not include a separate timeline board. It does not constrain the subsequently reviewed single-scene [Editor](#edit-a-scene). Multi-scene editing remains deferred; trimming, audio processing and transitions are still outside the current skill runtime (Q-01).

The r04 Shot-local tabs, r05 minimal tabs, r06 conversation-first layout, r07 Generate/Studio tabs and r08 two-product mockups are exploration history for this flow; see D-10 to D-14.

### Earlier workboard / Studio exploration — preserved context

The current working-view rules (removed: `navigation-options.md`) describe r04; the [saved r03 mockup](history.md#unmigrated-prototype-evidence) preserves the earlier study. The creator selected A, then questioned where actual single-Shot work happens. After discussing another agent's recommendation, they requested a mockup of a stable Studio with different deliverables presented in its canvas.

**Selected direction:** one persistent project workboard with all Shot rows grouped under Scene headings. Related material stays available in context rather than through disconnected generators. The first-release endpoint in Q-01 is still unresolved.

**Earlier r03 proposal, not the current r04 scope:** the Studio is the persistent workspace; the grouped workboard is one presentation. Assets / Shots / Preview stay in a stable header. Opening a Shot gives it the main canvas, with the same Shot's storyboard, candidate video and comparison views. Quick reference inspection uses a side panel; substantial asset work receives the full canvas. Scenes remain organization/optional focus, not a required page before Shot work. The r03 mockup uses existing local Gallery images with explicitly fictional Take/production states. Producing it does not approve the new layout or alter creative/runtime decisions.

| Surface | Proposed responsibility | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Workboard | Ordered Shot rows grouped by Scene hold the storyboard Panel(s), generation status and associated candidate/selected Takes. Preserve the visual plan when motion arrives | Replacing the Panel with a video and losing the approved plan, or presenting candidates as selected footage |
| Asset catalogue / inspector | Open a character, prop or environment sheet from a Shot's reference links, with its exact version and relevant usage. Allow full-sheet inspection without losing board position | A separate navigation journey merely to check a character, or silently substituting the latest asset version |
| Shot detail / Takes | Generate or revise motion for that Shot, compare alternatives and make an explicit Take decision. Batch actions can operate on a clearly stated group of ready Shots | A mandatory blank video-generator page where the creator must rebuild prompts and references |
| Sequence preview | Open a player from the same workboard to inspect the current ordered assembly, with missing Shots, draft candidates or selected material distinguished | Calling an incomplete assembly a final cut, or implying playback approves material |
| Contextual feedback / progress | Attach notes to the selected asset, Shot or sequence and show what is running or needs attention | Making chat history or a separate jobs dashboard the only way to understand project state |

In that r03 proposal, the Studio is the persistent working surface, and the board is one view; detailed controls appear when relevant. It need not show every asset sheet, Take and editing control simultaneously. A dedicated library or advanced view may still exist as an optional route, not a required happy-path detour.

**Video generation recommendation:** start it from the storyboard's Shot context within the workboard. An action such as “Approve storyboard and animate” could combine a clear creative decision with starting the already-requested next task; its consequences and scope must be explicit. Approval alone must not trigger unwanted production, and existing runtime gates are not removed by this design proposal.

**Asset lookup recommendation:** every Shot exposes links to the characters, props and environment versions it uses. Inspecting a sheet does not change it. Editing an asset surfaces affected Shots and decisions before a revision proceeds; it is not a global silent replacement.

**Preview recommendation:** show the sequence preview without leaving the workboard. Reserve “final cut” for an actually supported editorial/finishing state; whether that is in the first release remains open. A draft/candidate assembly and a selected assembly require distinct labels. Selecting a Take, approving a storyboard and watching playback remain different actions.

If motion is included in the chosen scope, a proposed minimal happy path is:

```text
Provide source + direction
    → review assets
    → review storyboard / start the requested animation work
    → review and select motion Takes
    → inspect the sequence preview
```

The agent handles preparation, reference wiring and routine execution between decisions. This is not a promise of a fixed click count or a single approval for all production. Changes, missing evidence and uncertain submissions may need focused intervention.

**Concrete design test:** while looking at one Mercer & Vale Shot, inspect a used asset, return to that same Shot, compare its Takes and preview the sequence without reconstructing context. If an asset change affects several Shots, show that impact rather than hiding it for the sake of fewer steps.
